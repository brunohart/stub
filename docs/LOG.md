## 2026-09-28 — Day 26: The fan, draw-downs and the swatch book

Same session, same day (brief §5.5–5.6).

**Shipped.** The bench's three words are buttons now, and each opens an object. **Movement** is a fan: this film in all eight movements, in your inks and on your stock, with your strip, fanned in an arc like a hand of cards (`FanLayout`, a custom `Layout`: 520-point radius, 32° of arc, 62-point cards, the one under the finger lifted 28 points and 1.18 larger, the way the Dock magnifies, with a selection tick as each card passes). The card the title's hash drew says "drawn from the title" underneath, and the model's would say "the model's". Let go on one and it flies to the bed (`matchedGeometryEffect`), lands, and the bed prints it again a pass at a time, 180 ms a pass, a light tap each and a heavy one for the foil. **Inks** are twelve draw-downs (`DrawDown`: each ink pulled across the palette's own ground, square where the knife started and ragged where it lifted) in a rail that snaps to one at a time (`scrollTargetBehavior(.viewAligned)`, the centred one a little larger through `scrollTransition`). Drag one onto the card and its inks flood out from where it lands; tap one and they flood from the middle. The flood is a new shader (`flood` in `Edition.metal`): the card in the new inks is revealed where a smoothstep on the distance from the drop point, pushed in and out by the stock's own noise, says so. It takes 0.55 s, with a wide soft edge on cotton and a crisp one on coated card, and when it has covered the card the inks are the proof's. Its reach is arithmetic, so a test holds it to covering every corner. **Stock** is a swatch book: four swatches lit by the same light as the bed, foil and holographic with their stamp. Hold one and rub it and you feel that stock under the finger (`Texture`); tap it and the card is printed again on it. VoiceOver: the fan is eight buttons ("Deco. Symmetry, a sunburst, stepped frames."), the draw-downs read as "Moss inks", the swatches as "Cotton. Thick, soft, uncoated." with a hint for double-tap-and-hold. Under Reduce Motion the fan does not magnify, the chosen movement cross-fades onto the bed, and the flood is a 0.2 s cross-fade. `run.sh --bench movement|inks|stock` and `--flood 0.4`; with `--bench movement --drive`, a finger slides along the fan and lets go on letterpress. Ninety-three tests.

Screenshots: `day-26-bench-movement.png` (the fan, the finger resting on deco), `day-26-bench-inks.png`, `day-26-bench-stock.png`, `day-26-flood.png` (night inks flooding The Brutalist, held at 0.4) and `day-26-fan.mov` (the slide along the fan, letterpress let go, the flight, the reprint; 3.1 MB).

**Reader.** No new seed. The season sentence is now cached from a model run ("You attended eight films in eight different cinemas…").

**Broke and fixed.** The first fan was a hand too big for the phone: 74-point cards on 40° of arc, turned about their feet, swung off both edges and past the bottom of the bench; 62-point cards on 32°, turned about their own centres (which sit on the arc, so the hand still pivots far below), fit with room to spare. As the fan closed, the three words slid from the top of the bench to its middle and doubled up for a moment; they are pinned to the top in every state now. The first flood test was a tautology (a number against itself plus something positive); `FloodEffect.reach` was made visible so the test could hold the real property. Visible, it crashed the test host: `FloodEffect` is a `ViewModifier`, which the SDK declares `@preconcurrency @MainActor`, so `reach` was main-actor by inference, the compiler let a nonisolated test call it, and Swift 6's runtime isolation check trapped inside its `map` (`EXC_BREAKPOINT` in `_swift_task_checkIsolatedSwift`). It is `nonisolated` now, which is what it is: arithmetic. The app only ever called it from `body`, on the main actor, so nobody using it could have hit this; the crash report macOS showed was the test host. `FanLayout` is safe the same way by declaration: `Layout` is `Sendable` and has no actor.

**Still rough.** The flight is a frame animation and does not carry the fan card's rotation, so the card straightens as it leaves. The chosen card lands whole and then the bed goes to bare stock to print it, a flash between the two that a direct hand-over of the print run would remove. Rubbing a swatch and feeling cotton against foil is a device test. The draw-downs are dragged with the system's drag and drop, which lifts after a long press; a drag that starts on a quick swipe would feel more like pulling a knife.
