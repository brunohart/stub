# Running Stub on a phone

Everything in this repo was proved in the simulator (ADR-003); a Release build for a phone compiles with no warnings (2026-09-30) but has not yet been signed or run on one. Some things cannot be proved in a simulator at all, and each needs one thing only Bruno can do. This page is the list.

## 1. Signing, and putting it on the phone

The team is in `project.yml` (`DEVELOPMENT_TEAM: 7CLZHA268P`, in the base settings so the app and the widget both get it; ADR-004: edit `project.yml`, never the `.xcodeproj`). Automatic signing does the rest the first time it reaches the account: it registers the phone, the two bundle IDs (`com.designedbybruno.stub`, `com.designedbybruno.stub.widget`) and the App Group (`group.com.designedbybruno.stub`), and issues a development certificate. The simulator build does not need any of it.

Three things only Bruno can do, once:

1. **Accept the latest Program License Agreement** at [developer.apple.com/account](https://developer.apple.com/account). Until then every provisioning request from Xcode or `xcodebuild` is refused with "PLA Update available: You currently don't have access to this membership resource", which is how the first signed archive failed on 2026-09-30. The same refusal shows the membership itself is live.
2. **Let Xcode sign in once.** Xcode › Settings › Accounts should list the Apple ID with the team `7CLZHA268P`. The development certificate in the keychain expired in May 2026; Xcode makes a new one on the first signed build.
3. **Pair the phone.** Plug it in, trust this Mac, and turn on Developer Mode (Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode; the phone restarts). After that it can be on the same network instead of the cable.

Then:

```bash
scripts/device.sh
```

It builds Release (how the feel, the shaders and the clip should be judged), signs it for the first paired iPhone, installs it and launches it. `--device "Name"` picks another phone. `--debug` builds the Debug app, which carries the fixtures and the launch flags, so `scripts/device.sh --debug -- -seed` files the nine fixtures on the phone. Never pass `-reset` to a phone whose drawer holds real stubs: it empties the drawer. Xcode's Run button does the same job: `xcodegen generate`, `open Stub.xcodeproj`, pick the phone, Run.

If the App Group is refused because the identifier is taken on another team, change it in both `entitlements` blocks in `project.yml` and in `SharedStore.swift` (`SharedStore.group`), then regenerate. Without the group the app still works: `SharedStore` falls back to the app's own Application Support and the widget shows an empty drawer.

Keep derived data outside iCloud: the scripts use `~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DerivedData/Stub-scripts` for the simulator and `Stub-device` for the phone (ADR-008).

## 2. Apple Intelligence on the phone

The on-device model needs Apple Intelligence on and its assets downloaded: Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri. Until then `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability` reports `.unavailable(.appleIntelligenceNotEnabled)` or `.modelNotReady`, the import screen's status line says so in words, and every stub is read by the heuristic parser (ADR-001). The launch probe (`ModelProbe`) asks the model for one word and caches the answer; when it answers, the seed log and every card say `by foundation-models`.

On this Mac the model answers in the iOS 27 simulator since 2026-09-28 (ADR-014), on the CPU fallback, so the eval's model columns are the simulator's and every *(device)* latency is still to be measured. If the probe runs out of its 20 seconds (a cold model loading for its first answer can), the status line says "On-device model slow to answer; asking it anyway" and the reader keeps asking the model, with the heuristic's draft to fall back on; before 2026-09-30 a timed-out probe locked the whole session to heuristics until the next launch. If the probe fails outright on a phone, the status line's reason is the one to read.

## 3. What to test on the phone, in order

Each of these is unproved in the simulator, for the reason given.

1. **The camera** (ADR-025). Press Keep a stub: the viewfinder should open straight away and ask for the camera the first time. Hold a real stub flat under it: the four marks should spring to the ticket's corners within half a second and ink orange with one firm tap when it is steady; tilt it away and they should let go. Take it: a paper flash, the frame holds, and the form comes up filling in, the plate cropped to the ticket. Try it in a dark room with the torch. Refuse the camera in Settings and press Keep a stub: the viewfinder should say so and offer Open Settings, with Photos and Type it in beside it. The simulator proved the marks and the reading on a still photograph (`run.sh --keep --camera fixtures/stub-4-past-lives.png --shutter`), never a live one.
2. **The App Shortcuts** (Day 5). Say "How many films this year in Stub" to Siri, or run it from the Shortcuts app. The simulator lists both phrases but cannot run them: `linkd` rejects the unsigned binary ("Unable to get teamId", then "Couldn't find AppShortcutsProvider"). "Log a stub in Stub" should open the app straight onto the import sheet.
3. **The widget** (ADR-027). Add The drawer to the home screen at all three sizes and to the lock screen. Medium and large: Another should deal the next stub with the card pushing in from the side, Turn should show the ticket as it was kept, and a tap anywhere else should open that stub in Stub. Keep a new stub in the app: the widget should start again on it. Then the home screen's tinted and clear styles (long-press the home screen › Edit › Customise): the stock should drop away and the edition's first plate and foil take the tint, the type still readable. The lock screen's rectangular and inline families show the film and its numbers only; on a locked phone they are redacted. The simulator proved medium on the home screen, dealing and turning.
4. **The icons at 60pt** (ADR-028). `scripts/make-icons.swift` draws ten; `build/icons/home.png` shows them at home-screen size on light and dark wallpapers, but a phone's screen is the judge. Open Your card (the brass-ringed button top right), open the icon drawer with a sandbox account, and put each icon on the home screen in turn; note any that read as a smudge at 60 points (the perforations on Scallop and Punched are the likeliest). Restore on a second device should open the drawer there too.
5. **The feel** (Day 2). The springs were tuned by arithmetic in the simulator. Press a card, hold the photograph in the detail, feel the haptic land when the plate slides into register. Reduce Motion (Settings → Accessibility → Motion) should remove the tilt and the overshoot everywhere. The buttons (ADR-029): press Keep a stub, The season, a tile under a card. The tap should land the instant the button goes in, not on release, and the release should spring back once and stop; the press-down spring is in `StubButtonBody`. Open the app cold: the cards should be dealt onto the table one after another from alternate sides, and land without a wobble; keep a stub and it should drop onto the table the same way.
6. **Streaming on a device** (Day 3). The simulator's model runs on the CPU fallback (`Cannot create MPS context`); the phone has the Neural Engine. The seed log's "Model streamed: title at … done at …" lines are the number to compare against the simulator's 3–18 s per stub.

7. **The edition in the hand** (Day 21, ADR-015). Open a stub. The card should lean up to eleven degrees as the phone turns and the light should slide across the foil and the stock with it; put the phone down flat and the card should settle square within a few seconds. Drag a finger slowly across the card: cotton should feel soft and dragging, foil and holographic bright and slick, and crossing the perforation should tick once. Tap to turn it over; hold the photograph on the back and the print should lift. Keep a new stub and feel the press: a light tap for each plate, a heavy one for the foil. With Apple Intelligence on, the first view of a release should say "Choosing an edition for …" for a few seconds and the detail's machinery line should say "chosen by the on-device model". Keep a new stub and check the same: since 2026-09-30 the season sentence waits for the edition, so the new copy's edition should be the model's, not drawn from the title because the sentence had the model. Reduce Motion should stop the card leaning and turning (the faces cross-fade) while the light still follows a finger. Keep a stub from a few years ago (type in its real date): its corners should be rubbed through the ink to the paper, and any foxing should come up through the ink but never through foil (2026-09-30; the simulator proves it with `--age 8`). None of this exists in the simulator: no gyroscope, no Taptic Engine, and foil only reads in light that moves.
8. **The wheel** (Day 24, ADR-016, ADR-022). Tap The press under the card (or hold the front until it lifts). The registration marks should come down part by part as the room opens and then rest fainter; tap inside one and the dial wakes. Open Movement and let go on one card after another: each should fly to the bed and print while the fan stays in the hand; the inked Movement stamp closes it. See the plates and press them together, slowly and with a flick: the type should not shimmer or trail at either end, and a flick should carry on with its own speed. Touch a part: everything else should knock back to a ghost. Turn the wheel slowly and feel one click a take, at the stock's sharpness (cotton dull, foil bright); spin it and the clicks should become a short ratchet rather than mush, then the wheel coasts, slows and settles on a take with one small overshoot. Past take 0 it should resist. The in-betweens should glide at 120 Hz on a ProMotion phone (`CADisableMinimumFrameDurationOnPhone` is set): check a fast scrub with Instruments' Hitches and SwiftUI templates, and note in the log whether any mark drops frames. The halftone already cross-fades rather than redrawing its dots each frame. With VoiceOver, the rotor on the card should list "Parts" (and, with the sheets pinched apart, each part's outline should sit on its own sheet, not where the part is on the flat card), and the wheel should be one adjustable element read as "Take fourteen, of ninety-nine". Under Reduce Motion the part should cut from take to take and never coast. Pinch the card apart: the sheets should lift in depth, the foil should still catch the light between them as the phone turns, and pressing them together should land with one heavy impact. Open Stock and rub each swatch in turn: cotton should drag and feel dull, foil and holographic should slip and feel bright. Open Movement and let go on a card in the fan: it should leave the hand at its own angle, turn square as it flies, its ink lifting as it goes, and land as bare stock that the bed prints pass by pass, with no whole card between the flight and the first pass (2026-09-30). Drag a draw-down onto the card and watch the edge of the flood: soft and wide on cotton, crisp on coated card. Pull the lever: it should push back harder as it goes down and give near the bottom with one heavy, dull knock (`Platen.ahap`), the card pressing a breath smaller and the ink wet for a couple of seconds, a gloss that follows the light as you tilt the phone and then dries. Shake to undo the pull. Sign the margin, shake, and the margin should be bare again; shake again and redo should sign it back (2026-09-30). Turn the card over and sign the margin with a finger; open another stub's proof and the same signature should be there, the same size.

9. ~~**The card on the table** (Day 29, ADR-018).~~ Cut on 2026-10-16 (docs/decisions/003-kill-list.md): its only way in had gone, it had never been tried on a phone, and it did not serve the moment.
10. **The moving share** (Day 30, BEDIP-221, ADR-022). Open a stub with a foil or holographic edition, tap Share under the card and choose "Share it turning in the light": it is 9:16 now, 1080 by 1920. Post it to a test Instagram Story and check that nothing of the card or the watermark sits under the Story's own controls at the top and bottom, and that the watermark ("shared in Stub", pressed into the paper under the card) is legible after Instagram's compression without shouting. "Share it turning, close up" is the card-sized clip. The share sheet should wait a moment while the clip is drawn (about ten seconds in a Debug build in the simulator; a Release build on a phone should be several times faster), then offer the three-second movie. Send it to yourself in Messages: the card should lean from one side to the other and back with no jolt at either end, the light crossing the foil as it does in the hand, the punches showing the parchment through them. "Share the still" should still send the still. Note the time from tapping to the sheet's destinations in the log; if it is long enough to feel broken, the clip can drop to 30 fps before anything else changes.

11. **Keep a ticket, from a link and from Shortcuts** (BEDIP-220, ADR-021). Send yourself the sample confirmation (`docs/add-to-stub/confirmation.html`, also at designedbybruno.net/stub/confirmation) and tap "Keep in Stub" in Mail: Stub should open on the import sheet with La Chimera filled in and "Handed over in a link." The association needs the signed app (the team's app ID is in the site's `apple-app-site-association`) and, on a Release build, Apple's CDN to have fetched the file; a Debug build from `device.sh --debug` reads it from the site directly. Then in the Shortcuts app, add Stub's "Keep a ticket" action, fill in a film and a seat, and run it: the same sheet, "Handed over by a shortcut." The simulator lists the action but cannot run it (`linkd` and the unsigned binary, as in item 2).

12. **Share a ticket into Stub** (BEDIP-218, ADR-023). In Wallet, open a real cinema pass, tap ⋯ › Share Pass and choose Stub: the sheet should show the film, the cinema, the time and the seat as the pass states them, with its strip. Send it, open Stub: the import sheet should be filled in, "Read from the Wallet pass", and nothing asked of the model. Do the same with a PDF e-ticket from Mail (share the attachment) and with a screenshot from Photos. Then, for the repo: export two real passes from two circuits (in Wallet, Share Pass › Save to Files), unzip each, replace the name, the booking reference, the barcode message and the serial number with invented ones, zip them again (`zip -X -r name.pkpass .` inside the folder) and put them in `fixtures/passes/` beside the synthetic three, with a test in `DigitalTicketTests` for what each should read. Note the circuits whose labels Stub did not know.

13. **One drawer, two devices** (BEDIP-219, ADR-024). First, the account: the first signed build with automatic signing should register the iCloud container `iCloud.com.designedbybruno.stub` on the team (check developer.apple.com › Identifiers › iCloud Containers, and that the app ID has iCloud with CloudKit and Push Notifications). Install on the phone and on a second device (or a simulator signed in to the same Apple ID). Keep a stub on one; within a minute or so (push permitting) it should be on the other, photograph and all. Pull a proof on the second; it should be on the first. Go back (delete the proof) on the first; it should go on the second. Turn Settings › Stub › Keep in iCloud off, open Stub, and the season sheet should say "Kept on this phone only". Before TestFlight: CloudKit console › the container › Schema › Deploy to Production, or a TestFlight build will not sync.

14. **Holding and filing** (ADR-026). Hold a stub in the drawer until it lifts: the cabinet's other labels should come down from the top, and moving it along its year should make the others move aside with a tick each; let go and it stays. Carry one onto another drawer's label: the label should open a little and thump; let go and "Filed in …" shows over the dock. Carry one past a year's divider: the cards of the other year should not move for it. Tap a year's tab: the stubs should slide down into the folder, the front coming up with a thud; tap again and they come back with a lighter tap. Switch Tickets to Editions: the cards should turn over in a wave from the front. Under Reduce Motion, none of it should travel, and VoiceOver's actions on a card should move it a place at a time.

## 4. TestFlight

Ready apart from the account. `scripts/archive.sh` archives a Release build for `generic/platform=iOS` into Xcode's Organizer and exports it with `scripts/ExportOptions.plist` (`app-store-connect`, automatic signing, symbols uploaded). Without arguments the signed `.ipa` lands in `build/export`; `scripts/archive.sh --upload` sends it to App Store Connect. The build number is the commit count (`CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION`, 149 at the time of writing), so an upload from a newer commit is always higher than the last; the version is `MARKETING_VERSION` in `project.yml`, `0.1.0`, shared by the app and the widget.

What the build already answers:

- **Privacy manifest.** `Stub/Resources/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` declares no tracking, no collected data, and one required-reason API, `UserDefaults` (the season cache and the printed editions, reason `CA92.1`). Nothing reads file timestamps, and the widget calls no required-reason API, so it needs no manifest of its own. It is in the built app's bundle (checked 2026-09-30).
- **Export compliance.** `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption: false` in `project.yml` answers the question on every upload.
- **The icon.** 1024 by 1024 with no alpha channel, as App Store Connect requires.
- **No fixtures.** The nine synthetic stubs are Debug only (`EXCLUDED_SOURCE_FILE_NAMES` in the Release configuration), so the Release app is 6.2 MB rather than 11.
- **Usage strings.** Camera and photo library are in `project.yml` (ADR-005). No network, no account, no third-party SDKs, so nothing in the app needs changing for review.

What only Bruno can do:

- **The icon drawer** (ADR-028). App Store Connect › the app › In-App Purchases: a non-consumable, product id `com.designedbybruno.stub.icons`, reference name "The icon drawer", a price near five dollars, Family Sharing on, a review screenshot of Your card with the icons, and the review note "Nine alternate app icons, chosen in Your card (the round button top right)." It needs the Paid Apps agreement (Business › Agreements) and banking and tax filled in, and it goes to review with the first build that sells it.

- **The App Store Connect record.** My Apps › + › New App: iOS, bundle ID `com.designedbybruno.stub` (it appears once the first signed build has registered it), SKU `stub`. The name must be unique on the whole store and "Stub" alone is probably taken; the home-screen name stays `Stub` whatever the store listing says (`CFBundleDisplayName`). Uploading from Xcode's Organizer (Distribute App › App Store Connect) offers to create the record if there is none.
- **Testers.** Internal testers (up to 100 on the team) see a build minutes after processing, with no review. External testers need a Beta App Review of the first build and a line on what to test; §3 is that line.
- **Screenshots** only for the store listing, not for TestFlight. The `docs/screenshots` set is at simulator resolution.
- **The app preview** (BEDIP-221). `scripts/build.sh && scripts/preview.sh` writes `build/preview/stub-preview-886x1920.mp4` (every iPhone with Face ID) and `stub-preview-750x1334.mp4` (iPhone SE), 25 seconds, filmed from the app. In App Store Connect, open the version › App Previews and Screenshots, and drop each on its display size. If it is refused for its bitrate or its audio, re-encode with `-minrate 10M` and a 256 kb/s track and note it in the log.
- **CloudKit** (ADR-024). CloudKit console › `iCloud.com.designedbybruno.stub` › Schema › Deploy Schema Changes to Production, after a development build has synced once; until then a TestFlight build keeps the drawer on the phone alone.
